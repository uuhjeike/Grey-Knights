This is much safer than putting 5 TB onto a single giant HDD.

A 6 TB WD Red Plus, for example, is a 3.5" SATA CMR HDD designed for NAS/home-office use; WD currently lists 6 TB versions with a 3-year warranty. 

Why I recommend this instead of RAID

Don't spend your limited budget on RAID first.

RAID 1 with two 6 TB drives gives you roughly 6 TB usable, but it is not a backup. If you accidentally delete a folder, malware encrypts it, or the filesystem becomes corrupted, the damage can be replicated to both drives. 

Instead:

Drive	Purpose	Connection

6 TB HDD #1	Main 5 TB data	Always connected
6 TB HDD #2	Complete backup	Connected only during backup
8 TB HDD #3	Disaster/archival copy	Normally disconnected + stored elsewhere


That gives you three physical copies.

The important trick: keep the backup disconnected

This is particularly useful for your situation.

For example:

Every week/month:

1. Connect HDD #2.


2. Synchronize/backup HDD #1 → HDD #2.


3. Verify the backup.


4. Safely eject HDD #2.


5. Disconnect it.


6. Store it somewhere physically separate.



Then periodically update HDD #3.

That means a ransomware infection, accidental deletion, electrical problem, or computer failure can't immediately destroy every copy.

The traditional 3-2-1 strategy recommends three copies, two media/storage locations, and one copy physically separated from the primary location.

I would not buy exactly 5 TB.

A 5 TB collection needs room for filesystem overhead and future growth. I'd choose:

Minimum:
6 TB + 6 TB + 8 TB

Better:
8 TB + 8 TB + 8 TB

The second option is much nicer if you expect your collection to grow.

You can run Linux + Samba and access the storage from Windows/Android over your local network.

No monthly fee.

The UPS protects the server/HDDs from abrupt power loss and unstable electricity. It doesn't make the data magically safe, but it reduces one important failure risk.

Your site is a profile page that builds itself from a plain text file, `posts.txt`. You write posts in that file and the page turns them into a feed.

## The three files

- **`index.html`** is the structure: the profile photo, the name "Grey Knight", the "Ordo Malleus / Daemon Hunters" line, the Share button, an empty feed area and a hidden fullscreen image viewer. It also holds the small sigil icons, drawn as vector shapes.
- **`style.css`** is the look: the dark theme, the arch frame around the photo, the smoke and ember background, and the layout for phones.
- **`script.js`** is the brain. It reads `posts.txt` and builds every post on the page.

## How a post becomes a card

1. When the page opens, the script loads `./posts.txt`.
2. It splits that file into posts. A line with only `-` marks the end of one post.
3. Inside each post, it looks at every line:
   - A line like `2026-10-07` or `2026-10-07 18:30` becomes the post date.
   - A line with only links becomes media.
   - Anything else is normal text.
4. Lines starting with `//` and anything inside `/* ... */` are ignored, so you can leave notes for yourself.

Here is an example of what you'd write in `posts.txt`:

```
2026-10-07 18:30
Today's transmission.
https://github.com/uuhjeike/Grey-Knights/blob/main/photo.png
-
Another post here.
```

## How links are understood

The script checks each link and decides what it is:

- **YouTube:** normal videos, Shorts and `youtu.be` links become an embedded player. It also keeps a start time such as `?t=30`.
- **GitHub links:** a `github.com/.../blob/...` link is quietly converted to its raw file address, so the image or video actually loads.
- **File type:**
  - Images (jpg, png, webp, gif and so on) show as pictures. Several images in a row become a gallery.
  - Videos (mp4, webm and so on) get a video player.
  - Audio (mp3, wav and so on) gets a custom player.
  - Files like pdf, zip and txt appear as a download card. Text-type files can show a preview.
  - Any other link is shown as a plain link.

## The extras

- **Loading in batches:** it shows 10 posts at a time (`BATCH_SIZE`) and loads more as you scroll, so a long feed stays fast.
- **Image viewer:** tapping an image opens it fullscreen, with Next, Previous and Close buttons.
- **Share profile:** this uses your phone's share sheet. If that isn't available, it copies the link and shows a small "toast" message.
- **Counters:** "Transmissions" and "Media" show how many posts and media items you have.
- **Safety nets:** if the profile photo or any media fails to load, it shows a fallback (the sigil or a "Media unavailable" message) instead of breaking.

## Where the photo comes from

The profile photo link is set at the top of `script.js`, in `PROFILE_PHOTO`. To change it, replace that link.

One thing to know: `posts.txt` isn't in the files you sent me. The site needs it in the same folder as `index.html`, otherwise the feed will show an error state instead of posts.
